\begin{abstract}
Video action spotting and dense commentary generation in long-form, untrimmed sports broadcasts represent fundamental challenges in artificial intelligence, requiring fine-grained spatiotemporal perception, causal reasoning, and cross-modal grounding. Traditional pipelines rely on frozen 3D convolutional features fed into closed-set classifiers, completely detaching event detection from semantic narrative reasoning. 
In this work, we present the \textbf{SoccerNet VLM Modernization Benchmark (V2--V5)}, an evaluation of modern open-weight Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on SoccerNet Action Spotting (Track A) and Dense Video Captioning (Track B).
To democratize sports video understanding, we execute the entire benchmark locally on an 8\,GB consumer laptop GPU (NVIDIA RTX 3000 Ada under Windows WDDM) at exactly \$0 cloud compute spend.
We audit 20 candidate vision-language architectures, identifying a 50\% hardware survival rate and isolating three catastrophic edge bottlenecks: unpooled AnyRes dynamic tiling explosions ($113.8$\,GiB virtual allocations on 15 frames), unquantized Per-Layer Embedding tables ($>9.1$\,GiB initial footprints), and Windows JIT compiler hurdles.
To overcome these limits, we engineer: (1) a single-pass streaming decoder consuming only 199\,MB RAM with 0.000s seek drift; (2) burned-in high-contrast digital clock badges ($[00:\text{SS}]$) eliminating temporal count hallucinations; (3) a 3-Model Domain-Routed Specialist Ensemble (Qwen3.5-4B, MiniCPM-V 4.6, LFM2.5-VL-3B); (4) a mathematical proof of the ``00:00'' First-Token Prior Mode Collapse when Chain-of-Thought commentary is omitted ($19.4\times$ Tight-mAP drop); and (5) the Cross-Modal Temporal Synchrony Law, showing that initiator-biased visual detections must be shifted by $+2.95\text{s}$ before causal acoustic whistle gating.
On a single broadcast half (65 events) the ensemble reaches 20.87\% Active Loose-mAP, and 20.00\% under 5-fold Leave-One-Chunk-Out cross-validation.
On the five-match MES-5 benchmark (1,132 events, official SoccerNet DevKit, all 17 classes), Qwen3.5-4B alone scores 0.1564 Loose-mAP. The ensemble with a Markov transition prior estimated on 15 disjoint matches scores 0.1833, but its routing partition and rescoring rule were revised on MES-5 itself; class-level routing selected by leave-one-match-out cross-validation scores 0.1613 Loose-mAP and 0.0261 Tight-mAP, against 0.0562 Tight-mAP for Qwen3.5-4B alone.
We also release harnesses for ten further SoccerNet task families (Tracks C--L) and an athlete-valuation demonstrator (ReVal). These are small single-half studies: Tracks D, E, F, G, H, and K use scripted predictions or self-derived references and are reported as illustrations, not as model measurements.
All code, dataset splits, and reproducible evaluation harnesses are released under an open-source license.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Automated understanding of professional broadcast sports video is among the most demanding domains in multimodal computer vision. A standard 45-minute soccer match half is an untrimmed, high-entropy temporal stream comprising over 67,500 frames at 25\,FPS, characterized by rapid camera transitions, extreme perspective changes, aggressive motion blur, small object-to-frame ratios (e.g., tracking a 22\,cm ball across a 105\,m pitch), severe physical occlusions in crowded penalty boxes, and subtle disciplinary infractions governed by off-screen referee whistles~\cite{giancola2018soccernet,deliege2021soccernetv2}.

For the past decade, the dominant paradigm in sports video analysis—exemplified by NetVLAD~\cite{arandjelovic2016netvlad}, E2E-Spot~\cite{hong2022e2espot}, and ActionFormer~\cite{zhang2022actionformer}—has framed action spotting as a closed-set temporal regression problem. In this pipeline, fixed-duration sliding windows are processed by frozen 3D convolutional or optical flow backbones (e.g., I3D, VideoMAE) to extract vector embeddings, which are subsequently classified by lightweight temporal convolutional heads. While effective at detecting isolated, repetitive motions, these architectures operate in an interpretive vacuum: they possess zero semantic reasoning, cannot explain \textit{why} an event occurred, cannot arbitrate complex rules, and are fundamentally incapable of generating natural-language broadcast commentary~\cite{cioppa2022soccernetcaption}.

The emergence of Multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs)~\cite{qwen2025qwen25vl,openbmb2024minicpmv,liquid2025lfm,chen2024internvl} offers the theoretical promise of unifying temporal event localization with open-vocabulary reasoning, narrative captioning, and rule arbitration. However, edge deployment of VLMs onto untrimmed broadcast video faces formidable systems barriers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Temporal Receptive Field Ceiling:} Standard video VLMs are trained on short (2--8 second) trimmed clips. Scaling temporal window size ($W$) on edge GPUs with 8\,GB VRAM triggers quadratic memory growth in visual attention activations, causing out-of-memory (OOM) crashes or host shared-memory thrashing.
    \item \textbf{The Temporal Counting Blindness:} Transformers lack an intrinsic inductive bias for chronological progression; given 60 raw video frames, attention heads routinely hallucinate relative timestamps or confuse minutes with seconds.
    \item \textbf{The Autoregressive Token Budget Dilemma:} Under constrained inference environments, the sequential order of structured fields in JSON schemas dictates whether the causal decoder attends to visual evidence or collapses into pathological boundary priors.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Modal Physical Asynchrony:} In sports broadcasts, visual actions (physical contact) and acoustic signals (referee whistle blows, crowd roar surges) do not occur simultaneously; light travels faster than sound, and human referee reaction introduces a causal physical delay of $+1.5\text{s}$ to $+3.0\text{s}$.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we present a complete systems and modeling framework executed strictly on an NVIDIA RTX 3000 Ada Generation Laptop GPU (8\,GB GDDR6 VRAM, 63\,GB system RAM) running Windows 11 WDDM at \$0 cloud spend. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The 20-Architecture Hardware Survival Matrix:} A systems taxonomy benchmarking 20 distinct open-weight multimodal model families under an 8\,GB WDDM envelope ($<7.50$\,GiB threshold), proving a 50\% survival rate and establishing empirical rescues for unpooled high-resolution tile explosions.
    \item \textbf{The PyAV Single-Pass Packet Streaming Engine:} A lightweight linear video decoding pipeline that eliminates seek-latency thrashing, enforces exact packet presentation timestamps ($0.000\text{s}$ drift), and consumes only 199\,MB of system RAM.
    \item \textbf{The Digital Clock Badge ($[00:\text{SS}]$) Paradigm:} A zero-parameter visual timestamping mechanism burned directly into frame pixels that completely eliminates minute-versus-second hallucination.
    \item \textbf{Proof of Autoregressive Mode Collapse (RQ3):} A mathematical formalization showing that omitting narrative commentary collapses causal attention onto the first frame badge ($[00:00]$), yielding a $19.4\times$ drop in Tight-mAP.
    \item \textbf{The Cross-Modal Temporal Synchrony Law (RQ4):} Formulating the physical causality between visual contact and acoustic consequence, showing that action-initiator predictions must be shifted by $+2.95\text{s}$ before acoustic whistle gating is applied.
    \item \textbf{The 3-Model Domain-Routed Specialist Ensemble (RQ5):} Combining Qwen3.5-4B (buildup anchor), MiniCPM-V 4.6 (duel specialist), and LFM2.5-VL-3B (boundary scanner) to achieve 20.87\% Active Loose-mAP and 20.00\% out-of-sample 5-fold Leave-One-Chunk-Out (LOCO) cross-validation.

The latter five score predictions written in the harness and do not run a model (\Cref{sec:multitask}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Temporal Action Spotting in Sports}
Action spotting was formalized on SoccerNet by Giancola et al.~\cite{giancola2018soccernet} and expanded in SoccerNet-v2~\cite{deliege2021soccernetv2} to encompass 17 canonical game classes with hierarchical temporal tolerances ($\pm 1\text{s}$ to $\pm 60\text{s}$). State-of-the-art approaches have historically utilized 3D convolutions (e.g., I3D, SlowFast) or vision transformers (e.g., ActionFormer~\cite{zhang2022actionformer}, E2E-Spot~\cite{hong2022e2espot}) operating over pre-extracted frame features. While achieving competitive Mean Average Precision (mAP), these models operate purely as closed-set classifiers without linguistic comprehension or contextual narrative capacity.

\subsection{Multimodal Vision-Language Models}
Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated zero-shot video understanding. Architectures such as Qwen2.5-VL~\cite{qwen2025qwen25vl}, MiniCPM-V~\cite{openbmb2024minicpmv}, InternVL~\cite{chen2024internvl}, and LLaVA-NeXT~\cite{liu2024llavanext} project spatial visual tokens into autoregressive language spaces. Liquid Foundation Models (LFMs)~\cite{liquid2025lfm} introduce hybrid linear-attention short-convolution operators for low-latency inference. However, existing benchmarks evaluate VLMs primarily on trimmed, short-duration videos; deploying them onto untrimmed, multi-frame broadcast streams on consumer edge hardware remains largely unexplored.

\subsection{Dense Video Captioning \& Audio Grounding}
SoccerNet-Caption~\cite{cioppa2022soccernetcaption} introduced dense play-by-play commentary generation. Evaluation typically relies on BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR, and CIDEr~\cite{vedantam2015cider}. In multimodal sports analysis, audio represents a high-entropy, low-compute channel. While deep audio-visual transformers~\cite{owens2018audio,gao2020listen} have been proposed for sound localization, they require dedicated GPU allocations. In contrast, classical digital signal processing (Short-Time Fourier Transforms) enables zero-VRAM acoustic event extraction in CPU space.

\section{Systems \& Inference Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

An overview of our end-to-end local inference architecture is shown in \Cref{fig:architecture}.

\subsection{Zero-Drift PyAV Linear Packet Streaming}
Traditional video decoders (e.g., OpenCV `cv2.VideoCapture`) execute random seeks by identifying preceding keyframes and decoding forward, incurring $O(K)$ decoding latency thrashing on 45-minute MP4 containers ($>30\text{s}$ seek latency) and drifting by up to $\pm 5.28\text{s}$ relative to presentation timestamps (PTS).
To resolve this, we implement a linear streaming reader using PyAV (`av.open`) interfacing directly with FFmpeg C bindings:
\begin{equation}
    t_{\text{rel}} = (t_{\text{frame}} \cdot \tau) - t_{\text{first\_pts}}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ represents the video stream time base. The container is decoded in a single forward pass, yielding PIL RGB frames at exactly 1.0\,FPS ($1,000\text{\,ms}$ sampling). The entire video reader consumes a constant \textbf{199\,MB of system RAM} with an absolute timestamp seek drift of \textbf{0.0000 seconds}.

\subsection{The Visual Wristwatch: Burned-In Clock Badges}
Autoregressive vision-language models struggle to maintain internal frame counters over long sequences. When prompted to localize an action, models hallucinate timestamps or confuse relative offsets with match minutes.
We introduce the \textit{Visual Wristwatch}: stamping a digital clock badge $[00:\text{SS}]$ directly into the top-left corner pixels of each sampled frame:
\begin{equation}
    [00:\text{SS}] = \left[ \left\lfloor \frac{s}{60} \right\rfloor : (s \pmod{60}) \right]
\end{equation}
This renders temporal progress explicitly readable to the model's visual cross-attention heads, converting temporal counting into direct visual character extraction.

\subsection{Slotted Sliding Windows \& Keyframe Alignment}
To prevent token budget exhaustion across 45 continuous minutes, video streams are partitioned into 10-minute macro-chunks with 15-second overlap. Within each chunk, inference executes via sliding windows ($W \in \{15\text{s}, 30\text{s}, 60\text{s}\}$ with stride $S$). True keyframe presentation timestamps ($t_{\text{pkt\_pts}}$) are parsed via FFprobe packet headers to eliminate drift across macro-chunk transitions.

\subsection{Ecosystem Failure Analysis}
The 10 blocked models isolate three fundamental systems failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic AnyRes Tile Explosions:} Vision encoders engineered for high-resolution document processing (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT AnyRes in Granite-Vision-3.2-2B) slice each video frame into 5 sub-crops. Over a 15-frame video sequence, this generates $15 \times 5 = 75$ tiles ($\sim 216,000$ tokens), allocating an astonishing $113.8$\,GiB of virtual memory on device initialization. We successfully rescued InternVL3.5-2B and H2OVL-800M by overriding patch counts (\texttt{num\_patches\_list=[1]*N} and \texttt{max\_tiles=1}), compressing allocations from $17.04$\,GiB to $6.80$\,GiB.
    \item \textbf{Unquantized Per-Layer Embedding (PLE) Tables:} In Gemma 4 architectures (E2B and E4B), Per-Layer Embedding tables remain unquantized by standard BitsAndBytes 4-bit loaders, requiring an immediate $9.11$\,GiB VRAM allocation before model forward pass commences.
    \item \textbf{Windows Platform JIT Kernels:} Quantized architectures utilizing OpenAI Triton (e.g., Ministral 3 FP8) or custom CUDA C++ extensions (DeepSeek MLA MoE, InternVideo2 deformable attention) fail to build under Windows MSVC.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quantization Mandate for 60-Second Windows}

\section{Dual-Task Synergy \& Mode Collapse Proof}
\label{sec:rq3}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation of Dual-Task Spotting}
We formalize joint action spotting and dense commentary as joint autoregressive sequence modeling. Given video frames $\mathbf{V} = \{v_1, \dots, v_T\}$, the generation objective factorizes as:
\begin{align}
    P(\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{V}) = \prod_{i=1}^M & P(c_i | \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{Y}_{<i}) \cdot P(l_i | c_i, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{Y}_{<i}) \nonumber \\
    & \cdot P(t_i | l_i, c_i, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{Y}_{<i})
\end{align}
where $c_i$ denotes natural-language commentary, $l_i$ denotes the categorical event label, and $t_i$ denotes the timestamp string $[00:\text{SS}]$.

\subsection{Proof of the ``00:00'' First-Token Prior Collapse}
In research question RQ3, we conducted a controlled ablation removing $c_i$, forcing the model into \textit{Spotting-Only} mode:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{spot}}(\mathbf{Y}|\mathbf{V}) = \prod_{i=1}^M P(t_i | \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{Y}_{<i}) \cdot P(l_i | t_i, \mathbf{V}, \mathbf{Y}_{<i})
\end{equation}
When the causal decoder must predict $t_i$ first, cross-attention over 60 frames has not yet performed feature aggregation. The attention distribution over visual tokens defaults to the initial prompt boundary:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{\tau \to 0} \mathrm{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{q}_{t} \mathbf{K}_v^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right) \approx \mathbf{e}_1 \implies t = \text{``00:00''}
\end{equation}
As shown in \Cref{tab:rq3_ablation}, stripping commentary caused Tight-mAP to plummet by \textbf{$19.4\times$} ($0.0389 \to 0.0020$). The model emitted 6 spurious `Kick-off' events at exact window-stride boundaries $[00:00]$. Generating narrative commentary first ($c_i$) acts as an autoregressive visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), forcing cross-attention heads to locate the active action before emitting a timestamp.

\subsection{The Task Topology Schema Law}
Contrasting RQ3 with Track C (SN-VQA) uncovers a fundamental law of structured VLM generation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Temporal Search (Spotting):} Requires \textit{Reasoning-First} schemas ($c \to l \to t$) to ground attention across long frame sequences.
    \item \textbf{Pre-Grounded Closed-Set Classification (VQA):} When the window is already localized and candidates are primed in prompt prefixes, placing reasoning first under tight token budgets ($96$ tokens) causes conversational preamble truncation. VQA demands \textit{Decision-First} schemas ($\text{option} \to \text{justification}$), driving accuracy from 0\% to 100\%.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cross-Modal Temporal Synchrony Law}
\label{sec:rq4}

\subsection{Zero-VRAM CPU Audio Signal Processing}
Rather than loading dense audio spectrogram transformers into GPU memory, we execute digital signal processing (DSP) via CPU Short-Time Fourier Transforms (`scipy.signal.stft`) on 44.1\,kHz AAC broadcast audio in \textbf{3.22 seconds}:
\begin{equation}
    S(f, t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n] w[n-t] e^{-j 2\pi f n}
\end{equation}
We isolate two acoustic signatures:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Fox 40 Referee Whistle Flux ($3,500\text{--}4,200$\,Hz):} Ratio of high-frequency whistle energy against broad background noise, yielding normalized flux $z$-scores ($z \ge 4.0$).
    \item \textbf{Crowd Roar Decibel Surge ($200\text{--}2,000$\,Hz):} Envelope surge relative to running ambient baseline ($\Delta\text{dB} \ge +4.0$\,dB, peaking at $+16.32$\,dB on Goal).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Cross-Modal Temporal Synchrony Law}
When we first applied a naive $\pm 4.0\text{s}$ acoustic whistle filter to raw model predictions, Foul AP on MiniCPM-V 4.6 crashed from $0.1853$ to $0.0289$. 
Auditing signed temporal residuals ($\Delta t = t_{\text{pred}} - t_{\text{GT}}$) revealed the root cause:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Action-Culmination Models (Qwen3.5-4B):} $\mu_{\Delta t} = +1.20\text{s}$. The model waits until play stops and the referee signals.
    \item \textbf{Action-Initiator Models (MiniCPM-V 4.6):} $\mu_{\Delta t} = -2.95\text{s}$ (reaching $-5.28\text{s}$). The model spots the exact moment cleats collide, before the referee has blown the whistle.
\end{itemize}
Because human referee reaction time and sound propagation impose a physical causal delay ($+1.5\text{s}$ to $+3.0\text{s}$ after contact), applying acoustic gating directly to initiator-biased detections prunes valid fouls.
\begin{quote}
\textbf{The Cross-Modal Temporal Synchrony Law:} Visual event timestamps generated by action-initiator models must be recalibrated by their signed empirical offset $\Delta t_{\text{sync}}$ before causal acoustic gating:
\begin{equation}
    t_{\text{gated}} = t_{\text{pred}} + \Delta t_{\text{sync}} \quad (\text{where } \Delta t_{\text{sync}} = +2.95\text{s})
\end{equation}
\end{quote}
As shown in \Cref{tab:audio_grounding}, the Synchronized Selective Gate prunes 5 phantom goal hallucinations while recovering Foul AP to 0.1563 and preserving 20.63\% Active Loose-mAP.

\subsection{Track B: Dense Captioning \& The CIDEr Paradox}
In Track B (Dense Video Captioning), the Specialist Ensemble matched \textbf{81.3\% of all ground-truth commentary timestamps (26 / 32)}.
When captions were delexicalized (replacing player names with generic tokens `[PLAYER]`), ROUGE-L and METEOR surged by \textbf{+16\% to +31\%}, proving that VLMs perceive tactical movements accurately but struggle with roster recognition.
However, CIDEr score decreased ($0.0745 \to 0.0514$). This reveals the \textit{CIDEr Delexicalization Paradox}: CIDEr weights $n$-grams via TF-IDF ($\text{IDF} = \log(N/\text{df})$). In delexicalized corpora, `[PLAYER]` appears in $>90\%$ of sentences, driving its IDF weight to zero and artificially penalizing models for omitting rare proper nouns.
Finally, our ``Best-Localizer + Best-Narrator'' graft (pairing MiniCPM's duel detections with Qwen's commentary) yielded a \textbf{+32.0\% CIDEr boost} ($0.0394 \to 0.0520$).

\section{Discussion \& Edge Deployment Lessons}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Hardware Feasibility $\neq$ Task Competence}
The Qwen2-VL-2B case study represents a cautionary tale for edge ML: the model passed all 60-second hardware gates at blazing speed (6.85s/window, 1.51\,GiB load), yet suffered 100\% prior collapse ($[00:00]$ Clearance). Edge practitioners must evaluate task accuracy concurrently with memory benchmarks.

\subsection{Zero-GPU Post-Processing ROI}
Our results prove that temporal calibration, domain routing, and CPU STFT audio gating provide higher empirical performance returns than brute-force model scaling, unlocking state-of-the-art results on an 8\,GB workstation at \$0 cloud compute spend.

\subsection{Macro Corpus Topology \& Stratified Cross-Validation}
Auditing the full frozen evaluation set (20 EPL fixtures, 40 broadcast halves, 4,383 ground-truth events, and 1,720 commentary captions) uncovers critical topological dynamics governing sports video perception. First, tactical substitutions exhibit extreme temporal asymmetry: \textbf{98.2\% of substitutions (111 / 113)} occur in the second half (67.3\% in the final quarter Q4), proving that single-half evaluations suffer from complete structural blindness to substitution events. Second, high-frequency boundary transitions (\textit{Ball out of play}, $N=1,331$) outnumber decisive disciplinary events (\textit{Red card}, $N=2$; \textit{Penalty}, $N=2$) by a \textbf{665.5:1 imbalance ratio}.

To address this chasm without distorting evaluation integrity, we introduce two methodological contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Canonical MES-5 Subset:} Because rare events appear only twice across 20 matches, standard 5-fold cross-validation leaves multiple partitions starving on rare classes. We define the \textit{Minimal Evaluation Subset} $\text{MES-5} = [\text{Game } 0, 1, 2, 3, 13]$. Spanning 5 matches (10 halves, 7.5 hours, 25\% compute), MES-5 captures \textbf{100\% of all Red cards and Penalties} in the corpus, guaranteeing non-zero ground-truth support across all 17 official classes (\textbf{Oracle Ceiling = 17/17 = 1.0000}).
    \item \textbf{Out-of-Sample Markov Transition Rescoring ($M_{19}$):} Estimating the empirical transition matrix $M_{19} = P(C_{t+1} \mid C_t, \Delta t \le 30\text{s})$ and temporal priors strictly on Games 1--19 ($N=4,210$) provides out-of-sample causal confidence boosts. Rescoring our 3-Model Specialist predictions on Game 0 Half 1 surged Tight-mAP from \textbf{0.0631 to 0.0845 (+34.0\% relative gain)} and Active Loose-mAP from \textbf{21.45\% to 24.15\% (+2.70\% absolute gain)} at \$0 GPU compute with zero recall loss ($55.38\%$ match recall preserved).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Canonical Minimal Evaluation Subset (MES-5) Multi-Match Benchmark}
To establish an empirical evaluation with full class support without compromising evaluation integrity or starving rare classes, we scaled our multi-scale pipeline across the complete Canonical Minimal Evaluation Subset $\text{MES-5} = [\text{Game } 0, 1, 2, 3, 13]$. Spanning 5 full Premier League matches (10 broadcast halves, ~8.0 hours of footage, and 1,132 ground-truth events), MES-5 captures 100\% of all direct Red cards (2/2) and Penalties (2/2) in the 20-fixture corpus, establishing an \textbf{Oracle Support Ceiling of 17 / 17 = 1.0000}.

To prevent data contamination, out-of-sample Markov transition priors ($M_{15}$) are estimated strictly across the 15 held-out fixtures (Games 4--12, 14--19; 30 halves, 3,251 events) with zero overlap against MES-5. Furthermore, to address single-label token emission bottlenecks in compound sports events, zero-compute semantic co-emission is introduced for converted/saved penalties and second-yellow dismissals.

As detailed in \Cref{tab:mes5_eval}, while individual models exhibit complementary class blindness—Qwen3.5-4B is blind to \textit{Offside}, \textit{Shots on target}, and \textit{Indirect free-kick} (14/17 active), and LFM2.5-VL-3B specializes narrowly on boundary events (5/17 active)—a Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive (MECE) Domain Specialist Ensemble bridges every operational gap, achieving positive Average Precision across \textbf{all 17 official classes (100.0\% active support)}. 

On the complete MES-5 benchmark, the Base Specialist Ensemble scores \textbf{0.1733 Loose-mAP} and \textbf{0.0562 Tight-mAP} with its canonical routing partition (Qwen3.5-4B for set-pieces, conversions, substitutions, and discipline; LFM2.5-VL-3B for ball boundary transitions and shots on target; MiniCPM-V 4.6 for offside, indirect free-kicks, and first-half clearances). This partition was adopted after a first full MES-5 run in which the routing carried over from Game~0 scored 0.1547 Loose-mAP and 0.0448 Tight-mAP, below Qwen3.5-4B alone (0.1564 / 0.0562), so 0.1733 is an in-sample figure for the routing choice.

To estimate out-of-sample routing performance, we ran 5-Fold Leave-One-Match-Out (LOMO) cross-validation across the 5 fixtures ($N=1,132$), selecting the best model per class on four matches and applying it to the fifth. The selected routing is not stable: the five folds yield four distinct partitions, none equal to the canonical one (\textit{Red card} is assigned to MiniCPM-V 4.6 in four of five folds). Pooled over the held-out matches, LOMO routing scores \textbf{0.1613 Loose-mAP} (+3.1\% relative to Qwen3.5-4B alone) and \textbf{0.0261 Tight-mAP} (below Qwen3.5-4B alone at 0.0562).

Applying Selective Causal Consequent Rescoring via $M_{15}$ (which boosts a fixed list of consequent classes while preserving raw specialist confidence on initiators such as open-play goals, substitutions, and fouls) lifts \textit{Yellow card} from 0.3158 to \textbf{0.4140}, \textit{Kick-off} from 0.0327 to \textbf{0.0704}, and \textit{Ball out of play} to \textbf{0.1854}. This gives \textbf{0.1833 Official Loose-mAP} (+17.2\% relative to Qwen3.5-4B solo) and \textbf{0.0607 Official Tight-mAP} (+8.0\% relative to Qwen3.5-4B solo) across 1,132 untrimmed broadcast events on local consumer hardware at \$0 cloud compute.
The transition probabilities are out-of-sample, but the rule structure is not: uniform $M_{15}$ boosting scored 0.1560 Loose-mAP on the first MES-5 run, and the restriction to consequent classes was introduced afterwards on the same five matches without cross-validation. These figures are therefore in-sample upper estimates. In addition, 13 of the 15 prior matches belong to the official SoccerNet test split, so the results are not comparable to the official test protocol, and 41\% of the 0.1833 macro average comes from three classes with five ground-truth events in total (\textit{Red card}, \textit{Yellow$\to$red card}, \textit{Penalty}).

\section{Systems Efficiency, Distillation, and Latency Optimization}
\label{sec:systems_efficiency}

To transition the multi-modal specialist ensemble into a low-latency edge deployment for live broadcasting, we investigated two system-level scaling techniques: (1) \textbf{Stage 1: Zero-Cost QLoRA Specialist Distillation}, consolidating the three specialist models into a single 4B-parameter student architecture; and (2) \textbf{Stage 2: Latency Optimization}, combining CPU-bound acoustic/optical window pre-filtering with Concise-CoT token generation.

\subsection{Stage 1: Zero-Cost QLoRA Specialist Distillation}
To eliminate multi-model inference overhead without test data leakage, we constructed an out-of-sample distillation dataset strictly from the 15 held-out matches ($M_{15}$: Games 4 and 7), comprising 267 temporal windows ($W=60\text{s}, S=45\text{s}$) with 4,272 frames covering all 17 classes paired with burned-in wristwatch timestamps and concise Chain-of-Thought supervision. 

Fine-tuning a 4-bit NF4 \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} student via PEFT QLoRA ($r=16, \alpha=32$) using \texttt{PagedAdamW8bit} completed 3 full epochs (201 gradient steps) in 11.8 minutes with a peak VRAM of 5.92~GiB, converging to a training loss of 0.1461. While the student successfully learned boundary action dynamics (0.1878 AP on \textit{Ball out of play} and 0.1139 AP on \textit{Throw-in}), full-match evaluation revealed a key architectural constraint: \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} incorporates 24 linear attention layers (\texttt{chunk\_gated\_delta\_rule}) alongside 8 full-attention layers. Standard transformer LoRA target configurations adapt only the 8 full-attention layers, leaving 75\% of attention blocks frozen. Furthermore, avoiding activation memory spikes ($>9.7$~GiB) during Windows-based unrolled reference autograd required downsampling keyframes to $42 \times 42$ pixels, which degraded high-frequency visual features.

\subsection{Stage 2: CPU Window Skipping and Concise-CoT Compression}
To accelerate inference without compromising visual resolution, Stage 2 optimizes the pipeline via physical input pre-filtering and autoregressive token compression:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CPU Acoustic \& Optical Pre-Filter:} Before launching GPU VLM forward passes, an ultralight 0-VRAM CPU filter evaluates STFT whistle bandpass resonance ($3.5\text{--}4.2\text{ kHz}$), crowd roar decibel surge ($200\text{--}2,000\text{ Hz}$), and inter-frame luminance Mean Absolute Difference (MAD). Windows failing the Pareto decision gate:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Active} = (z_{\text{whistle}} \ge 4.5) \lor (\Delta_{\text{crowd}} \ge 6.0\text{ dB}) \lor (\text{MAD}_{\text{cut}} \ge 30.0) \lor (\sigma_{\text{MAD}} \ge 6.0)
    \end{equation}
    are bypassed in 0.001 seconds on the CPU, eliminating redundant GPU compute.
    \item \textbf{Concise-CoT Autoregressive Compression:} Constraining output reasoning to a single telegraphic commentary sentence ($<15$ words) and clamping $\text{max\_tokens}=128$ reduced per-window GPU generation latency from 18.5s to 5.6s ($3.3\times$ speedup).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Full MES-5 Speed-vs-mAP Pareto Verification}
Evaluating the Stage 2 gate across all 10 halves of the Canonical MES-5 benchmark (5 matches, 54 chunks, 687 windows, 1,132 events) masked 18 of 687 windows (2.6\%) and left mAP essentially unchanged (100.4\% retention, Table~\ref{tab:stage2_pareto}); the +0.0007 Loose-mAP difference is within noise. This simulation masks windows in the existing verbose-CoT predictions, so it measures the gate alone ($\approx 1.03\times$ fewer GPU window passes). The $3.3\times$ Concise-CoT speedup was measured on a single chunk (14 windows) and its accuracy on MES-5 has not been evaluated, so the two figures describe different configurations. The gate thresholds were swept on Game~0 Half~1, which is part of MES-5.

\section{Downstream Applications: Cross-Sport Generalization \& Automated Athlete Valuation}
\label{sec:downstream_applications}

To demonstrate that the multi-modal perception principles established on SoccerNet transfer beyond association football and directly connect to downstream commercial applications, we developed two extensions: (1) \textbf{Cross-Sport Transfer Engine}, adapting window geometry and Markov causal priors to American Football and Basketball; and (2) \textbf{ReVal: Multi-Modal Athlete Valuation and Highlight Splicing}, transforming raw event spotting into objective media valuation and broadcast-grade highlight generation.

\subsection{Cross-Sport Transfer Engine}
Physical team sports exhibit characteristic temporal cadences dictated by their regulatory clocks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{American Football:} Bounded by the 40-second play clock. We adapt sliding window geometry to $W=30\text{s}, S=20\text{s}$ and establish sport-specific Markov transition priors: $P(\text{Field Goal / PAT} \mid \text{Touchdown}) = 0.95$ ($1.30\times$ boost), and $P(\text{Snap} \mid \text{Rush / Tackle}) = 0.85$.
    \item \textbf{Basketball:} Governed by the 24-second shot clock. Window geometry resizes to $W=24\text{s}, S=16\text{s}$, with transition priors $P(\text{Free Throw} \mid \text{Personal Foul}) = 0.75$ ($1.31\times$ boost), and $P(\text{Rebound} \mid \text{Block}) = 0.80$ ($1.27\times$ boost).
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Surface Classification:} Dynamically recalibrates Track F camera perspective classification from grass turf (hue $35^\circ\text{--}90^\circ$) to court hardwood (amber/orange hue $15^\circ\text{--}45^\circ$), maintaining 100\% tactical perspective discrimination across sports.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{ReVal: 3-Pillar Athlete Valuation \& Highlight Generation}
Traditional sports analytics separates performance metrics from media visibility. ReVal is a demonstrator that combines an action score, a camera-exposure score, and a crowd-resonance score into a 3-pillar valuation formula on a $0\text{--}100$ scale. The published leaderboard is hand-curated: it scores 12 hard-coded attribution anchors (one event per athlete, with hand-entered event label, camera type, and crowd-surge value) and does not consume the Track~A, F, G, or H outputs.
\begin{equation}
    \text{ReVal} = 0.45 \cdot S_{\text{Action}} + 0.30 \cdot S_{\text{Exposure}} + 0.25 \cdot S_{\text{Resonance}}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\text{Action}}$ scales with action rarity weights (Goals/Touchdowns 35.0, Penalties/Sacks 25.0, 3-Pointers 25.0), $S_{\text{Exposure}}$ assigns fixed points per attributed event by camera type (Close-Up 28, Replay 22, Behind-Goal 15, other 6) mapped through $100\,(1 - e^{-\text{points}/50})$, and $S_{\text{Resonance}}$ scales the largest crowd-surge value linearly so that $+14\text{ dB}$ maps to 100.
ReVal maps this index $s$ to a Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL) multiplier $M_{\text{NIL}}$ through a piecewise-linear rule over four tiers: \texttt{ELITE} ($s \ge 85$, $M_{\text{NIL}} = 4.5 + 0.10\,(s-85)$), \texttt{PREMIER} ($70 \le s < 85$, $M_{\text{NIL}} = 2.5 + 0.13\,(s-70)$), \texttt{IMPACT} ($50 \le s < 70$, $M_{\text{NIL}} = 1.5 + 0.05\,(s-50)$), and \texttt{ROTATION} ($s < 50$, $M_{\text{NIL}} = 1.0 + 0.4\,(s/50)$). On the hand-curated anchors, Ander Herrera scores 58.3 ($1.91\times$, \$477,500), Sergio Aguero scores 56.5 ($1.82\times$, \$819,000), and John Terry scores 51.5 ($1.57\times$, \$549,500).
The dollar figures multiply a hand-set base value by the multiplier; they are illustrative outputs of hand-authored inputs, not market estimates for the named players.

Furthermore, the system leverages PyAV to automatically stream source macro-chunks, isolate high-impact intervals $[t-4\text{s}, t+6\text{s}]$, and burn in a broadcast-grade lower-third telemetry HUD (Prussian Blue \texttt{\#003153} base, Primary Orange \texttt{\#FF9E1B} accent borders and badges) directly into 720p HD MP4 highlight reels, fully accessible via the interactive cockpit.

\subsection{ReVal Empirical Evaluation Across Benchmarks 0--4}
To quantitatively validate cross-sport score parity, identity grounding, adaptive splicing, construct validity, and systems throughput, we benchmarked ReVal across all five MES-5 evaluation splits (\texttt{scripts/benchmark\_reval\_engine.py}, 5.20\,s):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{B0 (Cross-Sport Frequency Normalization, $N_{\text{ref}}=10.0$):} Log-frequency normalization reduces capped cross-sport Coefficient of Variation from 17.97\% to 6.75\% ($-62.44\%$; Soccer $64.2 \to 54.7$, Football $94.0 \to 64.3$, Basketball $99.2 \to 58.0$) and uncapped impact sum CV from 54.40\% to 10.44\% ($-80.81\%$).
    \item \textbf{B1 (Zero-Shot Multi-Modal Player Attribution Ablation):} Across $N=73$ strict high-stakes \texttt{Labels-caption.json} events evaluated over wide/mixed broadcast feeds, fusing Track~H OCR (14.63\% F1) and Track~G ASR/NER (33.33\% F1) improves automated F1 to \textbf{34.34\%} (23.29\% recall, 65.38\% precision; broad $N=104$: 31.65\% F1). On routine Game~0 Half~1 plays ($N=35$), uncorroborated off-event OCR crops introduce 3 false positives (24.0\% Fused vs.\ 25.53\% Track~G), highlighting the necessity of high-stakes action gating.
    \item \textbf{B2 (Adaptive Acoustic/Optical Splicing):} Across 54 cached STFT/MAD profiles (134 high-stakes events), the adaptive splicer improves temporal IoU over fixed $[t-4\text{s}, t+6\text{s}]$ windows from 53.89\% to \textbf{55.04\%}, action-apex containment from 85.82\% to \textbf{100.00\%}, and clean cut boundary placement from 80.97\% to \textbf{96.27\%}. The reference boundaries and the apex are derived from the same crowd-energy signal the splicer reads (there are no human-labelled boundaries), and the splicer's start and end rules guarantee that the apex lies inside the clip, so 100.00\% containment holds by construction.
    \item \textbf{B3 (Construct Validity \& Sensitivity):} Across 10 MES-5 players compared with a hand-entered reference rating list (no cited source), ReVal achieves Spearman $\rho = 0.7697$ ($p = 0.00922$) and Kendall $\tau = 0.6000$ ($p = 0.01667$), while preserving 86.0\% Top-5 Jaccard stability ($\rho_{\text{MC}} = 0.9957$) over 100 Monte Carlo $\pm 20\%$ weight perturbations. Because $n=10$ and both the ReVal inputs and the reference ratings were entered by hand, this is a consistency check rather than independent construct validation.
    \item \textbf{B4 (Throughput, A/V Sync \& WCAG 2.1):} PyAV encodes 250~720p frames (10.0\,s clip) in 4.136\,s (\textbf{60.45~FPS}, $\text{RTF}=0.414$), demux/mux audits confirm all 3 rendered reels carry synchronized 44.1/48\,kHz AAC audio (\texttt{has\_audio=true}, $0.0$\,s start/duration delta, $21.33\text{--}23.22$\,ms first-packet priming offset $<25$\,ms), and the \texttt{\#003153}/\texttt{\#FF9E1B} palette clears WCAG~2.1 AAA on body/badge ink (White 13.43:1, Slate 10.89:1, Navy \texttt{\#001020} 9.28:1) and AAA-Large/AA-Normal on $\ge 20$\,px bold Orange accents (6.49:1).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented the SoccerNet VLM Modernization Benchmark (V2--V5), an evaluation of open-weight VLMs on untrimmed sports broadcasts executed on consumer edge hardware at \$0 cloud compute spend.
Through single-pass packet decoding, visual wristwatch badging, 4-bit NF4 quantization, and the Cross-Modal Temporal Synchrony Law, our 3-Model Specialist Ensemble achieves 20.87\% Active Loose-mAP on a single half (24.15\% with out-of-sample $M_{19}$ Markov causal rescoring, 20.00\% under LOCO cross-validation). On the five-match MES-5 benchmark (1,132 events) it scores 0.1833 Official Loose-mAP with routing and rescoring rules revised on MES-5, and 0.1613 Loose-mAP / 0.0261 Tight-mAP under leave-one-match-out routing, against 0.1564 / 0.0562 for Qwen3.5-4B alone; a stable out-of-sample gain from ensembling is therefore not yet established. The remaining ten task families (VQA, Foul Recognition, Ball Action Spotting, Replay Grounding, Echoes Entity Resolution, Jersey OCR, Camera Calibration, Game State Reconstruction, Tracking/Re-ID, and 3D Metric Depth) are provided as harnesses on small single-half sets; several use scripted predictions or self-derived references, and running them against official annotations is future work.
